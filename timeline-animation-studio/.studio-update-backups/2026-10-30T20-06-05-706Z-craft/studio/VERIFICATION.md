# Verification — Timeline Animation Studio Pro

The documentary workflow supersedes the initial infographic-first draft. Research: 26 YouTube videos selected from the exact search, 25 opening samples visually inspected, one unavailable. Full tutorial viewings are not claimed. Evidence and limitations are in `research/twenty/REVIEW_*.md`.

## Corrected documentary checks

`test-output/qa-documentary.json` records the actual automated results:

- Documentary is the first library and default project; 16 documentary presets, 36 secondary diagrams and 26 reference links load.
- All 52 project schemas validate, including embedded NASA photos, original transparent cutout art and documentary camera data.
- The photo world spans 5,110 reference pixels versus a 1,920-pixel composition. Camera position remains constant during a hold, travels before the next arrival and settles on the selected event.
- The ruler holds at 1969, passes through an intermediate value during travel and settles at 1981.
- Identical timestamps render identical frames. Hold edits, undo, portable JSON roundtrip, direct stage dragging and automatic stop construction work.
- Every documentary preset was rendered at five times across its duration. Four contact sheets and individual larger stills were visually inspected; revisions fixed cutout-side placement, header overlap and portrait framing.
- Green/blue backgrounds are exact on the source canvas; transparent corners have zero alpha; matte is grayscale across the tested families. A focused custom-colour matte check also passed.
- A real imported red-then-blue WebM backdrop was sampled at 0.2 and 1.4 seconds. The exported pixels were red and blue respectively. Separate preview/export video decoders prevent concurrent preview seeking from changing exact export frames.
- Browser MP4 H.264, WebM and transparent PNG-sequence exports complete. Cancellation was tested in the earlier shared exporter checks.
- 390px phone Library, Events, Edit and Export panels fit without document overflow; camera-stop controls are reachable. No browser exceptions in desktop/phone checks.

## Measured preview

On this session’s headless Chrome, warm 960×540 rendering across the 16 documentary presets stayed below 0.7 ms at the measured 95th percentile. A three-second sustained playback sample produced 181 frames with about 16.8 ms p95 frame intervals and preserved elapsed project time. These are measurements of this environment, not promises about a physical laptop or phone. The actual measurements remain in the JSON report.

Static preset thumbnails are bundled. Photos, background surfaces and artwork are cached. Preview uses screen-sized Auto/Fast/Full buffers; export resolution is independent.

## Real output checks

`test-output/production-verification.json` is generated by `qa-production.mjs` after rendering actual documentary clips and decoding them with FFmpeg. It records file sizes/streams, confirms silent outputs and verifies a decoded VP9 alpha mask has a transparent corner and visible elements. Browser MP4/WebM outputs are decoded as video, rather than merely checking file extensions.

The playable examples are `Documentary Photo Journey.mp4`, `Documentary Year Ruler.mp4` and `Documentary Cutout Timeline.mp4` under test-output. The journey includes a full sequence and final-wide context; the shorter ruler/cutout examples demonstrate travel between the first two events.

## Limits

- No physical phone was tested. Mobile Safari codec availability and long-video memory behaviour remain device-dependent.
- Perspective is simulated Canvas 2D depth/obliqueness, not a full After Effects 3D camera, optical depth of field or equivalent motion blur.
- Browser transparent video is delivered as PNG/PNG sequence; transparent VP9 WebM uses the PC renderer.
- Long 4K exports were not stress-tested. Browser encoding depends on hardware/browser codec support; use short ranges or the PC renderer.
- The demo NASA images are illustrative and have a source manifest. They are not all photographs of the exact year/event shown. Cutout spacecraft are original vector illustrations.
- Dense 100-event projects are supported as editable data, but no claim is made that all captions remain readable in an overview. Use camera visits or split scenes.

Earlier diagram/editor/export checks remain in `qa-report.json` and `export-decode.json`. Their performance numbers describe the earlier build; use the documentary report for the final main workflow.
