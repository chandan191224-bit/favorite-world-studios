## Patterns supported by the inspected frames

1. **Documentary media travel:** Sources 14 and 17 show a wide chronological world, with the visible camera region moving through it. Photos/video cards are large; only a useful neighborhood is shown at once. Main controls should cover camera stops, holds, travel duration, ticks, date size and media placement.
2. **Footage overlays:** Sources 11, 19 and 20 show timeline/date graphics over photographic or talking-head footage. A clean alpha/key element export is essential. A central pointer and contextual neighboring dates are repeated visual devices.
3. **Rolling date windows:** Sources 13 and 16 use masked vertical year lists, which require a different animation approach from horizontal rails. Large typography and a decisive settled date matter.
4. **Secondary media families:** Source 12 supports rough-masked dated-photo chapters; source 18 supports circular-photo processes. They should remain recognizable options rather than dictate the default documentary timeline.
5. **Evidence limits:** Contact sheets establish visible geometry, media hierarchy and coarse sequence changes. They do not establish sub-second easing, invisible keyframes, unseen later tutorial results or the audio behavior. No claims in this document depend only on source titles.
