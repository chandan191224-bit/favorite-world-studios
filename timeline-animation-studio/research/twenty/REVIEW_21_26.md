## 21. Worldo Adobe — Scrolling Timeline Year Animation in After Effects

- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aaLKwXjmaGc (69 seconds total).
- Evidence inspected: `aaLKwXjmaGc/contact.jpg`; approximate frames 1.5–34.5 seconds.
- What the animation actually is: **a horizontally scrolling strip of large year text**, not a line of milestone cards. At about 1.5 seconds, one year is leaving the crop while `2011` arrives; by 4.5 seconds, `2020` is isolated centrally on black. Later tutorial frames show the years assembled as one long horizontal text sequence (`2010 2011 2012…`) and a rectangular crop/mask region.
- Geometry and movement: the sequence extends well outside the visible composition. Only a small window of adjacent years is visible. Transition frames soften/motion-blur text as it traverses that window. No event-node connectors, branch diagram, or ring is present in the opening result.
- Documentary use: a short **year-change insert or overlay** that advances the narration to a new date, then settles on the year. A studio needs editable start/end years, direction, crop width, year spacing, travel time, settle/hold time, text colour, active-year emphasis and transparent/keyable output.
- Limits: the opening demonstration is short; the rest of the reviewed sample is setup UI. Later workflow steps and final interpolation settings were not inspected.

## 22. byl — Create EPIC Timeline Animations like Netflix in Minutes

## 23. PremiumBeat / Evan Abrams — After Effects Video Tutorial: 3D Timeline Animation

- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k9aVeJOHjcA (1051 seconds total).
- Evidence inspected: `k9aVeJOHjcA/contact.jpg`; approximate frames 1.5–34.5 seconds. The opening ~1.5–16.5 seconds contain the finished animation; later frames start composition setup.
- What the animation actually is: a **continuous offscreen chronological axis in a 3D gallery**, with a dense series of minor date ticks and larger periodically spaced markers. Large framed photos stand beside the rail. Bold date flags such as `Jan. 1987`, `Jan. 1989` and `Jan. 1990` sit near their corresponding pictures.
- Camera and staging: successive images move from a ship photo, past countryside and aerial imagery, to a leaning-tower photo. The photos are shown obliquely and at different apparent scales as the camera travels **along the rail**. The current photo occupies a substantial part of the frame; previous and next photos remain partly visible in depth. This is not an overview with every caption equally small.
- Documentary use: establish chronological direction, travel to the next dated image, pause with a readable photo/date, then continue. A studio needs rail-space/world-space separated from screen-space; perspective or simulated depth; editable camera travel/hold/zoom; current-event focus; date flags; media planes; per-event placement; and optional pull-back context.
- Limits: the sampled frames demonstrate clear camera progression but do not establish exact pause lengths or interpolation. Full tutorial workflow and expressions were not reviewed.

## 25. Motion Nations — Advanced 3D Documentary Animation in After Effects / 3D History Slideshow

- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HDf5JHhuMmI (1431 seconds total).
- Evidence inspected: `HDf5JHhuMmI/contact.jpg`; approximate frames 1.5–34.5 seconds. The finished example occupies roughly 4.5–28.5 seconds, between the opening creator shot and a logo/setup transition.
- What is visible: monochrome archival image planes stand in a dark, textured room/tunnel. Large red date/year text and bold white topic labels sit near the images, including a civil-rights scene, soldiers and an aircraft. The image is the dominant storytelling object. A subtle floor and repeating dark image planes add depth. No continuous tick ruler is visible in this sample.
- Camera and staging: at approximately 4.5–7.5 seconds the view pushes toward the civil-rights image; by 10.5 seconds it has travelled to a soldiers image. At 13.5–16.5 seconds it travels to an aircraft plane and moves closer. The sequence repeats later with more visible background image planes. Strong foreground/background size changes, lateral travel and push-ins create the chronology experience.

This should be an optional documentary scene treatment alongside a date rail, rather than being mislabeled as a year-ruler animation. Useful controls are media-plane spacing, foreground date/title placement, camera depth/zoom, image angle, dark texture and motion duration.
- Limits: only opening sampled frames were inspected; exact camera paths, easing, effect stack and the remaining tutorial are not established. Date labels visible in a tutorial demonstration are not treated as fact-checked historical claims.

## Shared conclusion for the rebuild

The strongest documentary references in this batch are **the travelling 3D date rail (23), the footage-plus-ruler overlay (22), and the scrolling year strips (21/24)**. Their essential behavior is a camera/view window travelling across information that extends beyond the screen, followed by meaningful date/event stops. A fit-everything milestone infographic does not reproduce that behavior. Decorative corporate-gallery staging (26) is secondary and should not define the default studio.
